1) BRAND ACCOUNT - Light Water Capital (LinkedIn)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
An oil-supply shock is supposed to be the one unambiguously good thing that can happen to an oil stock. This week, that reflex broke.

The Iran war moved from rhetoric to infrastructure: the U.S. destroyed five Iranian tankers, strikes set fires at Saudi energy facilities including a ~400,000 b/d refinery at Jazan, and Iran ran its heaviest day of Hormuz shipping attacks yet. Brent pushed back above $100.

The commodity did exactly what you'd expect - crude, Brent, and gasoline all jumped about 6%. The energy equities did not follow. The sector fund fell, oil services dropped more than 2%, and the majors barely moved. A six-point gap between the barrel and the stocks that own it, in a single session.

That gap is the story. When crude rips 6% and the market marks the producers down, the equity is telling you something the price alone can't: the move isn't being read as good news. Triple-digit oil is two forces at once - a windfall for the barrel, and a tax on the economy that consumes it. Past ~$100, high prices start to destroy demand: people drive less, freight and factories pull back, discretionary spending thins. By refusing to chase the stocks, the market voted for the tax.

Gold confirmed it. A war panic lifts gold; this sold it - down 1.7%, with the miners off 3.5% and long bonds falling too. With a hot producer-price print the same morning (wholesale diesel +24%), the oil shock read as higher-for-longer, not as fear. Real yields up, not a flight to safety.

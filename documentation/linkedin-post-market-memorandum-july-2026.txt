Market Memorandum: Supply Scare, Demand Reality

The Iran ceasefire collapsed this week. The U.S. struck Tehran, Brent spiked 5.4% to $78, and for a moment it looked like February all over again.

Equities shrugged it off — the S&P closed near its highs, on track for its fourth gain in five weeks.

That's not complacency. It's a read on demand. The IEA now projects the first annual decline in global oil demand since the pandemic. Every rally in crude is getting sold into weakening demand.

But the supply side hasn't gone away — the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at 319.5M barrels, down 23% and the lowest since 1983. The buffer that would normally absorb a supply shock isn't there anymore.

Our latest Dispatch memorandum, written by Quant Strategy Intern Suriya Narayanan Rajavel, breaks down what's setting the price of oil right now, the risk scenario if the demand-side reprieve doesn't arrive in time, and the signals we're watching next.

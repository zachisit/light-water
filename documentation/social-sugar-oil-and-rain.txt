Raw sugar has climbed from a February low near 13.3 cents a pound to about 21.3 cents in mid-October, its highest since early 2025. Hardly anyone saw it coming.

Three things did the work. Expensive oil pulled Brazil's cane toward ethanol early in the season. Bad weather, much of it from a strengthening El Nino, cut crops in Brazil, India, Thailand and Europe in the same year. And hedge funds swung from a net short of about 232,000 contracts in February to a net long of about 260,000.

For two weeks now the market has gone sideways on thinner volume, and the March-May premium has narrowed from 0.81 cents to 0.65. We break down which of the three legs still has fuel and what would settle the next move:

I've been watching sugar curl on the daily as volume dries up, so we dug into what built this rally. The answer is oil, weather and a big fund short that had to be bought back. Most of the short covering is done, and by late September sugar was already paying Brazil's mills more than ethanol. That leaves weather and India's policy as the things to watch. A quiet range near the highs can go either way, and the curve is already starting to lean.

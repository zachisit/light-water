Boring Wins: DCA vs. Buying the Dip

There's a rule that makes the rounds every time the market gets ugly: hold cash, wait for the Nasdaq to drop 10%, back up the truck on your favorite names, repeat once a year — that's how you get rich.

We backtested it. On QQQ, 1999 to today.

The bounce is real — buying a 10% dip beats the average day handily at three and six months. But the rule skips the part that matters: a standing weekly buy that never looks, no timing, no cash sitting on the sidelines, finished with roughly twice the wealth of the dip-timer. Same contributions, same 27 years. The dip-buyer sat in cash 96% of the time, and time in the market was the whole game.

There's also a trap hiding in "your favorite stocks" — the index self-cleans and recovers; a single speculative name that falls 10% on its way to zero does not.

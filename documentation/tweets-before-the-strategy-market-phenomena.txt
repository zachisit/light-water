OPTION 2 - SINGLE TWEET, THE METHOD
================================================================

A test I've started leaning on: shuffle a stock's history within that stock, so every observation survives but the sequence is gone. Anything predictive after that can't be about timing.

Run it on volatility clustering and most of the effect survives, which isn't good news.

Across seven phenomena tested on US equities back to 2004, variance came out somewhere between 23 and 354 times more predictable than direction, depending on the horizon.

OPTION 4 - THREAD (7 tweets)
================================================================

1/
Last week we tested the seven market behaviours that strategies are usually built on top of, instead of testing a strategy: momentum, volatility clustering, shock response, volume, resiliency, commonality and correlation. US equities back to 2004, each against its own null.

2/
Two corrections did most of the work.

The first is to shuffle each stock's history within that stock, keeping every observation but destroying the order. Anything still predictive afterwards can't be about timing, because there's no timing left in the data for it to use.

3/
On volatility clustering, between 70% and 87% of the effect came through that shuffle depending on horizon.

So most of what looked like memory was really a fairly stable ranking of which names are jumpy, which is a different and much less useful thing to know.

4/
The second correction is for overlapping windows. A 120-day rolling correlation calculated today shares 119 of its 120 days with yesterday's, so consecutive readings aren't close to independent, and treating them as if they are overstates the evidence badly.

5/
We ran 155 tests of the claim that correlation and dispersion persist, and none of them survived that correction. In one case the same statistic over the same window went from p = 0.000 to p = 0.888.

6/
What did hold up was the gap between variance and direction, with variance 23 to 354 times more predictable depending on horizon.

Sizing off a 20-day ATR forecasts a rank correlation near 0.62. Filtering entries on 20-day momentum gets you about -0.016.

7/

OPTION 2 - SINGLE TWEET, THE METHOD
================================================================

The most useful test we ran this year:

Shuffle a stock's history within that stock. Every observation survives, only the order dies.

Whatever is left cannot be about timing.

Run it on volatility clustering and most of the effect is a roster of permanently jumpy names.

OPTION 3 - SINGLE TWEET, THE ASYMMETRY
================================================================

Variance is between 23 and 354 times more predictable than direction, depending on horizon.

That is why volatility targeting survives contact with live markets and most direction filters do not.

You can size on what you can forecast. You cannot steer on it.

OPTION 4 - THREAD (7 tweets)
================================================================

1/
Before testing a single strategy, we spent six days testing the seven market behaviours strategies are built on top of.

Momentum. Volatility clustering. Shocks. Volume. Resiliency. Commonality. Correlation.

US equities back to 2004, each against its own null.

2/
Two corrections did nearly all of the damage.

The first: shuffle each stock's history within that stock. Keep every observation, destroy the order.

Anything that survives that cannot be about timing. It is just the stock being what it always is.

3/
Run that against volatility clustering.

Between 70% and 87% of it, depending on horizon, is not a forecast at all.

It is a permanent roster of jumpy names. The effect was real. The interpretation was wrong.

4/
The second correction: overlapping windows.

A 120-day rolling correlation computed today shares 119 of its 120 days with the one computed yesterday. Those are not independent observations, and most tests treat them as if they were.

5/
We tested 155 claims that correlation and dispersion persist.

Zero survived.

One p-value moved from 0.000 to 0.888 on the same statistic over the same window. The only thing that changed was counting the sample honestly.

6/
What did hold up is the asymmetry.

Variance is 23 to 354 times more predictable than direction.

Sizing off a 20-day ATR forecasts a rank correlation near 0.62. Filtering entries on 20-day momentum forecasts negative 0.016.

That is not a weak edge. It is the absence of one.

7/
We also logged eighteen defects in our own measuring tools. Ten would have returned a confident wrong answer with no error raised at all.

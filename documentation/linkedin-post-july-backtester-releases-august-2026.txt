Twelve Bugs We Found in Our Own Engine

July Backtester shipped three releases this month. The newest is a correctness release, and it is not a comfortable one to publish: twelve verified bugs in our own engine, each fixed on its own branch with its own tests.

The through-line is that most of them were found by checking a claim rather than by reading code — several were hiding behind a check that already reported clean. Our data-quality validator matched column names literally, so against a lowercase corpus it skipped instead of failing: a frame carrying a High-below-Low violation, a 400% price jump and thirty zero-volume bars scored 100 out of 100. The same validator scored a newest-first frame 100.0 and the identical data in ascending order 55.0.

August also brought survivorship-free point-in-time universes built from rules rather than index membership, cross-sectional rotation as a first-class strategy type, and stops that anchor to the signal bar rather than to the fill.

Two people did a great deal of that work.

Shardul Mehetar, our Quantitative Strategy Intern on the Systematic Strategies team, made the universe liquidity and history screens genuinely point-in-time, taught the price-jump check to weigh magnitude instead of counting occurrences, and built the tooling that reads a posted comment back and diffs it against what was sent.

Suriya Narayanan Rajavel, an open-source contributor to Light Water, built the parquet data layer and the Norgate and Polygon pipelines that the whole survivorship-free effort runs on top of.

An engine you can check is worth more than an engine you have to trust.

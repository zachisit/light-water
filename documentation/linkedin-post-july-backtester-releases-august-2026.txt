A Month of Improvements to July Backtester

Three releases went out in August, and the direction across all of them was the same — make it easier to ask a question of the data, and easier to trust the answer.

Universes are now survivorship-free. You can build one from rules — liquidity, price, listing status — rather than from index membership, which most corpora simply do not carry. That clears a long-standing blocker on point-in-time work.

Cross-sectional rotation is a first-class strategy type now, with a rotation-aware engine path and a worked example, rather than something each researcher assembles themselves.

Stops can anchor to the structure of the signal bar instead of a fixed distance from the fill, which is far closer to how the levels are actually chosen.

Norgate and Polygon can be read through a single merged provider. And a new research index collapses an entire run history into one sortable table, so "which experiment actually worked" becomes a sort rather than an archaeology exercise.

We also put real time into correctness this month: an audit of the engine, stricter data-quality validation, and tighter checks around risk sizing.

Two people did a great deal of this work, and they deserve the credit publicly.

Shardul Mehetar, our Quantitative Strategy Intern on the Systematic Strategies team, made the universe liquidity and history screens genuinely point-in-time, taught the price-jump check to weigh magnitude rather than count occurrences, and built the tooling that verifies what it posted actually landed.

Suriya Narayanan Rajavel, an open-source contributor to Light Water, built the parquet data layer and the Norgate and Polygon pipelines that the whole survivorship-free effort runs on top of.

July Backtester is open source, and free to run on Yahoo Finance data with no API key.

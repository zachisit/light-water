Chips broke first. Everything else followed.

On Thursday the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.2%, the Nasdaq 5.5% (its biggest one-day drop in 19 months, per Reuters) and the S&P 500 4.4%. The chip fund SMH had its second-worst day in our data back to 2003, behind only March 16, 2020.

How the day unfolded:

- It started overnight, in chips only. Korea's SK hynix fell 9.5% and Japan's Advantest 14.7%. By 9:25 a.m. Eastern, SMH was down almost 4% while the S&P 500 was flat.

- After the open, it spread to everything. Every S&P 500 sector fell. By late morning, even the defensive staples and utilities funds that had been rising turned lower.

- The makers were the target. Micron and AMD fell about 17% and Lam Research 16%, on roughly three times normal volume. The cloud giants that buy their chips fell 2% to 4%.

What didn't happen matters as much. Long-term Treasuries rose, the dollar was flat, and high-yield bonds fell only 0.3%. That looks like a repricing of AI chip stocks, not a crisis in the financial system.

One more signal: the VIX closed above every VIX futures contract, the deepest such reading since April 2025. Tom McClellan has called this a "bottoming condition," while noting it is not the same as the exact bottom.

Our full read, including what we can't tell yet and what we're watching before Nvidia reports on November 17, is in the first comment.

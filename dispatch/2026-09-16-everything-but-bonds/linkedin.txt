The Fed raised rates for the first time in three years on Wednesday, and the tape reacted like it mattered. The dollar caught the cleanest bid of the session, gold and the energy complex sold off hard, and stocks slipped - the full fingerprint of a genuinely hawkish surprise.

The new dot plot backed it up. The 2026 median rose to 4.1% from June's 3.8%, and 2027 held flat at that same level, erasing the rate-cut path the Committee had penciled in earlier this year. Chair Warsh was direct in his press conference: "This summer's inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved."

The one place that didn't confirm the hawkish story was the place most exposed to it. Long Treasuries rallied into the 2pm statement, sold off hard once Warsh started taking questions, and still closed green on the day - a curve move that got stronger the longer the duration.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said the central bank has "more work to do" on inflation.

The bond market yawned.

His first Jackson Hole keynote was genuinely hawkish - the 2% target is "firm and fixed," financial conditions "hard-pressed" to call restrictive. Markets came in braced for it, pricing roughly one-in-three odds of a September hike. Then almost nothing happened: yields moved about half a basis point, Treasuries and the S&P finished near flat.

The one market that actually traded the message was the dollar - up against all eight majors.

Our read on why the non-reaction is the signal, and the lesson that's reusable for every Fed event: a central banker's words only matter to the extent they move real yields. On Friday, they didn't.

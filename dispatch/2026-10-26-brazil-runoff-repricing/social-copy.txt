1. BRAND ACCOUNT - Light Water Capital (LinkedIn)
========================================================================
U.S.-listed Brazil assets were already sharply lower in New York premarket trading on Monday, hours before Brazil's own stock market opened.

The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ) printed 19% below Friday's close at 4:00 a.m. Eastern and was down 31% fifteen minutes later. It opened the regular session 25.5% lower, the largest opening gap in our data, which goes back to 2003. It closed down 19.8% on 299 million shares, the most it has traded in a day in that history. In Sao Paulo, B3 halted trading with a circuit breaker at the open, and the Ibovespa closed down 13.9%.

Three things stood out:

- Brazil was the outlier. The real fell almost 8% against the dollar. The Mexican, Chilean and Colombian pesos moved much less, and so did the Mexico, Chile and Peru country funds.

- The currency mattered. At the close, translating EWZ's dollar loss into reais implies a decline of roughly 13%, so currency translation accounts for roughly a third of the dollar loss. Unhedged Brazil equity funds carry that currency exposure.

- The stocks split broadly by exposure. XP fell 48% and several banks and payment companies lost far more than the exporters: Bradesco -28%, StoneCo -27%, Itau -20%. Vale fell 3% and Suzano rose 2%.

EWZ recovered about a fifth of its opening gap by the close. That is consistent with a liquidity or positioning effect in a thin premarket, but prices alone can't tell us why it rebounded.

Our full read, including what the data can't show yet and what we're watching next, is in the first comment.

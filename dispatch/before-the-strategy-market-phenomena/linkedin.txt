Before testing a strategy, we spent six days testing the seven market behaviours strategies are built on top of.

Momentum. Volatility clustering. Shocks. Volume. Resiliency. Commonality. Correlation. US equities back to 2004, each one tested against its own null hypothesis rather than against a backtest.

Two corrections did most of the damage.

Shuffle each stock's history within that stock - keep every observation, destroy the order. Anything that survives cannot be about timing. Run that against volatility clustering and 70% of it at twenty days, and 87% at one year, turns out to be a permanent roster of jumpy names rather than a forecast.

Then correct for overlapping windows. A 120-day rolling correlation today shares 119 of its 120 days with yesterday's. We tested 155 claims that correlation and dispersion persist. Zero survived. One p-value went from 0.000 to 0.888 on the same statistic, same window.

What did hold up: variance is between 23 and 354 times more predictable than direction. That asymmetry is why volatility targeting works and why direction filters mostly do not.

We also broke our own measuring tools eighteen times. Ten of those would have produced a confident wrong answer with no error at all.
